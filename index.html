Help and privacy

Even Sync sends what you leave on under What Even Sync sends: today’s steps, resting heart rate, heart rate variability and exercise minutes; your sleep from recent nights, or up to a month on the first sync; your alarms; and the titles, times and calendar names of today’s and tomorrow’s events from the calendars you keep. Anything with no data is left out, and anything turned off that still arrives is discarded.

Your device key is saved as a plain-text file, even-device-key.txt, in an Even folder in On My iPhone. It isn’t encrypted by Even. Anyone who can open that file, and any Shortcut you let read it, can use the key. Don’t share it.

If Shortcuts asks, turn on Allow Sharing Large Amounts of Data in Settings → Apps → Shortcuts → Advanced.

To sync on a schedule, add a Time of Day automation in Shortcuts that runs Even Sync, and choose Run Immediately. Your iPhone must be unlocked to read Health data.

To disconnect, tap Disconnect this iPhone, then delete the file in Files.

Privacy

Your first name can appear in notifications, including on your Lock Screen. Mood check-ins stay on this iPhone unless you turn on check-in sync, which saves them and their notes to your account. Health data, calendar events and alarms you sync are sent over an encrypted connection and stored in your private account. They aren’t end-to-end encrypted. You can disconnect a device or erase synced data at any time.

How the Shortcuts work

Even Sync is what everyone installs. It sends Health and calendar data. Running it from this app connects it, so there’s no key to copy.

Even Release is for developers. It holds no credentials, and only approved accounts can publish.

To release a change: push it, then tap Run Release Helper. It keeps itself up to date, runs Even Sync and checks the versions. If an update is needed, tap Replace and run it again. If a version needs publishing, it asks you first and tells you when it’s live.
